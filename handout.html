AIU Compass

The problem

AIU hosts students from more than 40 countries. The same conversation fails every week at the registrar, the clinic, the dorm office and the cafeteria: the student knows what they need, but not the room, the office or the hours — and not in the language the counter speaks.

What it does

One tap, ask in Thai, English, Indonesian, Vietnamese or Chinese. It resolves campus vocabulary that general translators get wrong (IT208, “ทะเบียน”, “dorm fee”), answers in the same language with the hours, and flies a campus map to the building.

Evaluation protocol: five questions, scored for room, office, hours, route and offline availability. Results are written in at the demo — no number is printed until it is measured. Office hours were confirmed on 25 November and cafeteria, clinic and church hours on 26 November; library and gym hours remain placeholders.

How it is built

  • Runs entirely in the browser: no app, no server, no install.
  • 31 places across 13 buildings, Thai and English names, hours, aliases.
  • Vocabulary layer: room-code regex, alias match, fuzzy match, Thai consonant skeleton.
  • Falls back to typed input, then to the cached pack, then to a recorded run.

Tested, and not yet tested

  • Tested: 35 automated checks (vocabulary layer, campus data), 17 browser checks, the offline cache, and both fallbacks: network down, microphone denied or missing.
  • Not yet: the five-question comparison, and speech on the demo phone — each has a named check and a blank result.

Next

A four-week pilot at one counter — registrar or clinic — measured by time-to-serve and repeat questions. Then IPITEx 2027, Class G (ICT), with the campus record extended to every faculty building.

Try it in ten seconds: scan the code, tap the microphone, say “ห้อง IT208 อยู่ไหน”. Then try English: “where do I pay the dorm fee”.
